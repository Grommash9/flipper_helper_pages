How it works

How scrap silver value is calculated

Melt value = weight in grams × fineness × silver price per gram. A 100 g sterling item contains 92.5 g of pure silver, so its melt value is 92.5 times the price of one gram of pure silver.

The fineness number in a UK hallmark is the parts of pure silver per thousand. The Birmingham Assay Office lists the common UK standards:

  • 800 — 80% silver
  • 925 — sterling silver, 92.5%
  • 958 — Britannia silver, 95.8%
  • 999 — fine silver, 99.9%

The same page notes that hallmarking is a legal requirement in the UK for silver items of 7.78 grams and over, so an unmarked heavy piece needs checking before you treat it as solid silver.

The live price is the silver spot price in pounds per troy ounce, loaded by your browser from gold-api.com when you open this page. The calculator divides it by 31.1034768, the number of grams in a troy ounce. You can overwrite the price with any figure you like.

Before you scrap it

Melt value is the benchmark, not the price

  • Buyers pay less than melt value. A scrap buyer or refiner keeps a margin. Get two or three quotes and compare each one with the melt value above.
  • Check resale value first. A hallmarked antique, a named maker or a complete set often sells for more than its weight on eBay. Search sold listings before you melt anything.
  • Weigh accurately. Remove stones, glass liners and non-silver parts, or weigh them separately. Loaded candlesticks and knife handles contain filler that isn’t silver.
  • Plate isn’t hallmarked silver. EPNS means electroplated nickel silver: a base-metal item with a thin silver coating. This calculator doesn’t value plate.
FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How do you calculate the scrap value of silver?

Multiply the weight in grams by the fineness (0.925 for sterling) to get the grams of pure silver, then multiply by today’s silver price per gram. That is the melt value. A scrap buyer pays a share of it, so ask for a quote and compare it with the melt value.

What is 925 silver worth per gram in the UK?

925 means 92.5% silver, so one gram of sterling contains 0.925 g of pure silver. Its melt value is 0.925 times today’s silver price per gram in pounds. The price moves every day, which is why the calculator loads the live price.

What do the numbers 800, 925, 958 and 999 mean on silver?

They are fineness marks: parts of pure silver per thousand. 800 is 80% silver, 925 is sterling (92.5%), 958 is Britannia silver (95.8%) and 999 is fine silver (99.9%).

Can I use this calculator for silver plate or EPNS?

No. EPNS means electroplated nickel silver: a base-metal item with a thin silver coating. The calculator works only for solid silver with a fineness mark.

Where does the silver price come from?

Your browser loads the live silver price in pounds per troy ounce from gold-api.com when you open the page. If the price can’t load, you can type a price per gram yourself.
