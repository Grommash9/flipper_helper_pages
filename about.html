Why this exists

Why I built FlipperHelper

Valentina started reselling in early 2025. Her first month target was £200 turnover. Every time she hit a target she doubled it for the next month. Within a year she had gone from charity-shop test runs to regularly clearing £1,300–1,900 a month.

The turning point was car boot sales. Trains do not work for early-bird boots, so she got a car — she was not driving before, and I taught her. Her business now pays all the car expenses.

For about a year she tracked everything in a spreadsheet, and the spreadsheet kept getting in the way: a buy logged at the stall, then a forgotten listing, a missed fee, per-item maths gone wrong. FlipperHelper went live on the App Store on 21 March 2026 — free, offline-first, multi-currency, tracking 16 selling platforms.

"If a feature doesn't survive a Sunday morning at a car boot, it doesn't ship."

Valentina is the lead tester, and she is the actual reseller. She came to the UK first and I followed; she worked at Costa until reselling started bringing in real money, then went full-time. She is the one with the eye for stock — I am the one who builds the tools. Most of the photos on the blog are from her sourcing trips.

The business

What we actually do

Most of the posts on this site are write-ups of one of these six things.

Where the stock comes from

  • UK car boots

    Surrey, Suffolk and north London, Sundays mostly. Hardstanding venues through the winter.

  • Charity shops

    The original learning ground. Now mostly for specific brands and ceramics.

  • France

    We drove via Eurotunnel for our first sourcing trip in spring 2026 and came back with a car full of vintage. We have been back since.

Where it goes

  • eBay, Vinted and Facebook

    eBay for collectors and high-value items, Vinted for clothing, Facebook Marketplace for furniture and local pickup.

  • Vintage events

    We book stalls at Wimbledon-area vintage markets. Useful for shifting inventory that was not moving online.

  • Shiply for furniture

    Shipping furniture to UK buyers, usually £40–£100 per piece.

The blog

About this site

The blog is where I write up what we learn as a UK reseller couple. Topics tend to fall into three groups.

  • Where to source — UK car boots, UK antiques fairs, Portobello Road, Saint-Ouen and French brocantes.
  • How to run the trips — driving to France for stock and UK online auctions.
  • The boring but profitable bits — Shiply for furniture delivery, tracking profits and the HMRC trading allowance.

There are also free tools that answer a single question in the browser, and a FAQ for questions about the app itself.

Background

My software background

The short version, for anyone wondering who is writing the code.

I have been a professional software engineer since before moving to the UK. My day-job work has been in tech and finance, mostly Python on the backend, with detours into Flutter, Swift and various data-engineering stacks.

FlipperHelper is built natively for iOS in Swift and SwiftUI and for Android in Kotlin and Jetpack Compose, with a local data store, OAuth integration with Google Drive for image export, and CSV export to Google Sheets. Open-source bits and other projects are on my GitHub.
