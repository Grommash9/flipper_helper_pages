How to Sell on eBay Australia (2026): Free Selling, Buyer Protection & Prepaid Labels

14 June 2026 Selling 13 min read

Since 12 May 2026, Australia-based sellers with up to $25,000 in eBay sales over the past 12 months pay no transaction fee on eBay.com.au. Buyers pay a Buyer Protection fee on top of your price instead. In exchange, you generally must send items with a label from eBay, and from 12 November 2026 eBay is rolling out prepaid Australia Post labels with postage worked out by eBay.

Update, 13 November 2026: an earlier version of this page said that free sellers pay about 2.4% + $0.30 in payment processing, that the Buyer Protection fee is “up to 8% + $0.30 per transaction”, and that eBay Store subscriptions are separate from the Pro plans. None of that matches eBay’s pages. Every figure below was checked against eBay Australia, Depop and ATO pages on 12 and 13 November 2026.

What changed on 12 May 2026

  1. Free selling. eBay says: “From 12 May, we’ve introduced free selling for Australia-based sellers with up to $25,000 in sales in the past 12 months.” Eligible sellers pay no transaction fee (final value fee) when an item sells.
  2. Buyer Protection fee. Buyers pay it when they buy from an Australia-based seller who has no Pro plan. It is a tiered percentage of the item price plus $0.30 per item. It does not come out of your payout.
  3. Labels from eBay. Sellers in free selling “will generally need to buy a label on eBay to post their items”. eBay let sellers use prepaid satchels they already had until 1 June 2026. After that date, eBay says you “may still be able to use your satchels” for items that are exempt from labels.
  4. Pro plans. eBay moved every Store subscription to the matching Pro plan on 12 May 2026. For example, an Anchor Store became a Pro Anchor plan. A new Pro Starter plan has no monthly cost.

Before the change, eBay says, “casual sellers would typically pay 13.4% transaction fee + $0.33 per order fee.” On a $50 item, that is at least $7.03 (13.4% of $50 + $0.33).

Sources: eBay AU, Important changes to eBay selling; eBay AU, It’s now free to sell; eBay Seller Centre, Recent selling changes

Who qualifies for free selling

eBay’s fee page lists three conditions. You must:

  • have a registration address in Australia;
  • sell without an eBay Pro plan (this includes Pro Starter);
  • sell AU $25,000 or less on eBay in the past 12 months.

Since November 2026, you also need an eBay balance account to receive your sales proceeds (see “Getting paid” below).

eBay says that eligible sellers pay fees only “for optional listing upgrades or if you deliver to an overseas address”. The fees that can still apply:

The Buyer Protection fee

The buyer pays this fee when they buy from a seller who has an Australian registered address and no Pro plan. eBay calculates it per item:

  • a flat $0.30 per item, plus
  • 8% of the item price up to $20, plus
  • 6% of the part of the price from $20 to $500, plus
  • 4% of the part of the price from $500 to $5,000.

No fee is charged on the part of the price above $5,000. The fee does not apply to Vehicles or Classified Ads.

  • You see the buyer’s price when you list. eBay shows the total that the buyer pays. You can edit that total, and eBay adjusts your item price.
  • Refunds. A full refund to the buyer also refunds the whole fee. A partial refund refunds the same share of the fee.
  • Pro sellers. No Buyer Protection fee is added to listings from sellers with a Pro plan.

Postage: eBay prepaid labels

eBay’s label help page says: “Starting from 12 November 2026, we are launching an improved prepaid label experience for Australia-based sellers who are enrolled in free selling.” Once eBay turns it on for your account, it updates the shipping options on your listings, and you must use the prepaid label for eligible items sent within Australia. Until then, you still generally need to buy a label on eBay.

How the prepaid label works:

  • When you list, eBay suggests a package size and weight. Check that they suit your item. Additional charges can apply if you declare the wrong weight or size.
  • eBay calculates the postage from the buyer’s address. You choose whether the buyer pays it or you offer free postage. With free postage, you pay one flat rate based on the parcel weight, wherever the buyer is.
  • When the item sells, you get a prepaid Australia Post label. Print it on A4 paper, or show the QR code at the post office and the staff print it for you.
  • Tracking uploads automatically. You can’t see the buyer’s address on the order page or upload tracking yourself.
  • If you send the item with the label and Australia Post scans it, you are protected if the parcel is lost or damaged. eBay handles eligible ‘item not received’ and return requests with the buyer.

Items that are not eligible for the prepaid label:

  • items listed for over $750;
  • items sold for less than $40 that can go in an envelope, such as trading cards, comics, coins, stamps, CDs, DVDs and video games;
  • heavy or bulky items. Prepaid label parcels can be up to 105 cm on the longest side, 0.25 m³ and 22 kg;
  • orders where the buyer chose local pickup.

Getting paid

Without a Pro plan, payouts are on demand only: you ask eBay to pay your available funds to your bank account. Pro sellers can also get daily, weekly, fortnightly or monthly payouts.

Free selling now requires an eBay balance account. eBay’s pages give two start dates: the fee page says 3 November 2026, and the eBay balance page says 12 November 2026. With eBay balance, you can withdraw your funds to your bank account or use them to pay for eBay purchases.

Sources: eBay AU, Getting paid for items you’ve sold; eBay AU, eBay balance; eBay AU, Selling fees without a Pro plan

How the $25,000 limit works

eBay says the $25,000 “is an eligibility criteria, not a fee-free allowance”. Once eBay moves you to Pro Starter, every new sale carries fees, not only the part above $25,000. How eBay counts:

  • Past 12 full calendar months. For example, the check in August uses sales from 1 August to 31 July.
  • All eBay sites. eBay adds up your sales on every eBay site, not only eBay.com.au.
  • Total amount of each sale. This includes the item price, postage and any tax. A $40 item with $10 postage counts as $50, so 500 such sales use up the limit.

If your total is over $25,000 and you have no Pro plan, eBay tells you on the 20th of the month. It moves you to a Pro Starter plan from the 1st of the next month. If your sales in the past 12 months are $25,000 or less, you can cancel a Pro plan and move to free selling. The change starts on the 1st of the next month.

What Pro Starter costs

Pro Starter has no monthly cost. eBay says “the final value fee is 13.4% for most categories”, and a fixed fee per order applies too. The fee is charged on the total amount of the sale, including postage. Pro Basic, Pro Featured and Pro Anchor have a monthly subscription and lower final value fees. See the exact rates on eBay’s Pro selling fees page.

A Pro plan gives you things that free selling does not:

  • no Buyer Protection fee on your listings;
  • your own choice of carrier, including prepaid satchels;
  • scheduled payouts;
  • multi-quantity listings and listings with variations;
  • volume pricing, seller coupons and third-party integrations (Pro Basic and above).

A seller with less than $25,000 in sales can choose a Pro plan. If you need these tools, compare the fees with what you would save.

Sources: eBay AU, Selling fees without a Pro plan; eBay AU, Pro subscriptions; eBay Seller Centre, Recent selling changes

How to list your first item

Step 1

Photograph the item against a plain background.

Take 4–8 photos. Include every flaw, so the buyer knows what they get.

Step 2

Title:

Start with the brand, item type, size, colour and condition. Example: Levi’s 501 Original Vintage Jeans Mens 32 Mid Wash Made in USA.

Step 3

Set category

Pick the category with care, because the item specifics change with the category.

Step 4

Item specifics

Fill in every field that applies. Buyers use these fields as search filters.

Step 5

Condition

Be honest. “Used” with photographed flaws is better than “Like new” with hidden issues. A very high rate of ‘Item not as described’ returns can add a 5.5% fee.

Step 6

Price

Check recent sold listings (filter by “Sold items”), not active listings. eBay shows the total the buyer will pay, with the Buyer Protection fee. You can edit that total.

Step 7

Format

Buy It Now with Best Offer suits most items with a known value. An auction can suit a rare item with no clear price. Multi-quantity listings need a Pro plan.

Step 8

Shipping

Check the package size and weight that eBay suggests. Choose whether the buyer pays postage or you offer free postage.

Step 9

Return policy

Choose whether you accept returns and who pays for return postage.

What to sell on eBay Australia

Ideas that resellers who source from op shops and garage sales often list:

  • Vintage clothing — Levi’s, band tees, Australian labels.
  • Vintage homewares — Pyrex, Bessemer, mid-century glassware.
  • Books — series, old Penguins, Australian sports annuals.
  • Toys and collectibles — Hot Wheels, vintage LEGO, Star Wars figures, NRL and AFL memorabilia.
  • Retro electronics — old consoles, games and film cameras. Test them before you list.
  • Spare parts — appliance parts and hard-to-find components.

Check the label rules before you buy stock. Cheap envelope items such as cards, coins and stamps under $40 are not eligible for the prepaid label, and nor are heavy or bulky items. For those, you choose how to send them. For prices and sourcing ideas, see Best Items to Resell in Australia.

Common mistakes Australian eBay sellers make

  • Treating $25,000 as an allowance. Over the limit, eBay moves you to Pro Starter and fees apply to every new sale. Check your total for the past 12 months each month.
  • Forgetting the Buyer Protection fee when you compare prices. Your $50 item shows as $53.70 to the buyer. A Pro seller’s $50 item shows as $50.
  • Sending with another service when the prepaid label is required. Your payment can be held for up to 14 days, and eBay won’t protect you if the parcel is lost.
  • Declaring the wrong size or weight. Additional charges can apply.
  • Selling overseas without checking the fee. A 3% international sales fee applies to the total sale, even in free selling.
  • Pricing from active listings. Active prices show what sellers hope to get. Sold prices show what buyers paid.

Tax: do you need to declare eBay sales?

The ATO separates a business, an income-producing activity that is not a business, and casual sales of unwanted personal items. It says: “It is important to remember that ‘not being in business’ does not automatically mean amounts you receive are tax-free.” (ATO, online selling)

The ATO’s own example is a person who buys items to resell at a profit and is not carrying on a business. Even so, “the amounts earned are assessable income that must be declared in their tax return.” Selling your own unwanted items is usually not assessable income.

If you are in business, you must register for GST when your GST turnover reaches $75,000 (ATO, registering for GST). This is not tax advice. Speak to a registered tax agent if you are unsure.

FAQ

Is it free to sell on eBay Australia in 2026?

Yes, if you have an Australian registration address, no Pro plan and up to $25,000 in eBay sales over the past 12 months. Since 12 May 2026 you pay no transaction fee. Fees still apply for optional listing upgrades and for delivery outside Australia (3%). You also need an eBay balance account to get paid.

How is the eBay Australia Buyer Protection fee calculated?

The buyer pays $0.30 per item, plus 8% of the price up to $20, 6% of the part from $20 to $500 and 4% of the part from $500 to $5,000. On a $50 item it is $3.70. It does not come out of your payout.

Do I have to use eBay’s prepaid labels?

In free selling, generally yes, for eligible items sent within Australia. Exceptions include items listed over $750, envelope items sold for under $40, heavy or bulky items and local pickup. To choose your own carrier, you need a Pro plan.

What happens if I sell more than $25,000?

eBay tells you on the 20th of the month and moves you to a Pro Starter plan from the 1st of the next month. Pro Starter has no monthly cost, but the final value fee is 13.4% for most categories, plus a fixed fee per order.

Is eBay Australia cheaper than Depop Australia?

For an eligible seller, eBay takes no fee on a domestic sale. Depop takes 2.6% + $0.30 payment processing. Buyers pay a fee on both: on a $50 item, $3.70 on eBay and up to $3.50 on Depop.

Do I pay tax on eBay sales in Australia?

Selling your own unwanted items is usually not assessable income. If you buy items to resell at a profit, the ATO says the amounts are assessable income, even if you are not in business. Speak to a registered tax agent if you are unsure.
