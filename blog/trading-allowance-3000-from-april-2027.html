The Trading Allowance Rises to £3,000 from 6 April 2027: What UK Resellers Need to Know

12 November 2026 News 8 min read

The short answer: from 6 April 2027 the trading allowance rises from £1,000 to £3,000. The Self Assessment reporting threshold for trading income rises to £3,000 at the same time. For the current tax year, 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027, the allowance is still £1,000.

HMRC describes this in the policy paper Income Tax: increasing the trading allowance, published with Budget 2026 on 28 October 2026. This post explains what the paper says and what it means if you buy stock to resell on eBay, Vinted, Depop or at car boots. For the full rules that apply today, see our guide to the £1,000 trading allowance.

This post explains HMRC’s published documents, checked on 12 November 2026. It is not tax advice. If you are close to a threshold, talk to an accountant or HMRC.

What changes on 6 April 2027

The paper says the measure “will have effect from 6 April 2027, for the tax year 2027 to 2028 onwards.” Here is what changes:

Rule 2026–27 (now) 2027–28 onwards
Tax-free trading allowance £1,000 £3,000
Self Assessment reporting threshold for trading income £1,000 £3,000
Flat deduction you can take instead of your actual expenses Up to £1,000 Up to £3,000

Sources: HMRC policy paper, 28 October 2026; HMRC, Tax-free allowances on property and trading income

In the paper’s words, the change will “increase the amount of relief provided by the allowance to £3,000, alongside increasing the Income Tax Self Assessment reporting threshold to £3,000.”

The government made an announcement at the tax update in spring 2025. It said that the reporting threshold for trading income would rise to £3,000 “by the end of this Parliament”. The October 2026 paper says the allowance itself rises too, and sets the date: 6 April 2027.

What the paper does not mention

The paper says that the Finance Bill 2026-27 will change the amount of the allowance. The rules for the allowance are in Chapter 1, Part 6A of the Income Tax (Trading and Other Income) Act 2005. The paper describes no change to these rules from HMRC’s current guidance:

  • The allowance applies to sales, not profit. HMRC says: “Gross income means the total amount you would put on your tax return before any allowances or expenses are taken off.”
  • You choose the allowance or your expenses, not both. “You cannot deduct any other expenses or allowances if you claim the allowances.”
  • You still keep records. “If you use the trading or property income allowances you must keep a record of your income.”
  • Some people must register even below the allowance. Examples in HMRC’s guidance: you made a loss and want to claim relief, or you want to pay voluntary Class 2 National Insurance.
  • Some income cannot use the allowance. You cannot use it in a tax year if you have trading income from any of these:
    • a company that you or someone connected to you owns or controls
    • a partnership where you or someone connected to you is a partner
    • your employer or your spouse’s or civil partner’s employer

The paper does not cover who counts as trading. HMRC’s online platforms guidance says you are probably trading if you sell goods that you bought intending to sell for a profit. If you sell your own personal possessions, “you probably do not have to pay Income Tax on these.”

The dates that matter for resellers

  • Sales from 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027 (tax year 2026–27): the £1,000 rules apply.
  • 5 October 2027: the registration deadline. If your gross trading income in 2026–27 is more than £1,000 and you are not registered for Self Assessment yet, register by this date. HMRC: “If your gross income for a tax year is more than £1,000, you must register for Self Assessment by 5 October in the following tax year.”
  • 31 January 2028: the deadline for the online tax return for 2026–27, and for paying the tax you owe. HMRC’s deadlines page gives 31 January 2027 for the 2025–26 return. The 2026–27 return is due one year later.
  • Sales from 6 April 2027 (tax year 2027–28): the £3,000 rules apply.

So the new allowance does not help with your 2026–27 return. If you sold more than £1,000 of stock in 2026–27, you still report it for that year.

Worked examples

These examples apply HMRC’s current rules with the new amount. The figures are made up to show the arithmetic. “Costs” means stock, postage, packaging and travel to sourcing.

Example 1: £800 of sales a year

  • 2026–27 and 2027–28: the allowance covers all of it. You do not need to register for this income, unless one of HMRC’s special cases applies to you.
  • Keep records either way.

Example 2: £2,400 of sales, £600 of costs

  • 2026–27: sales are over £1,000, so you need a tax return. With the allowance: £2,400 − £1,000 = £1,400 taxable profit. With actual costs: £2,400 − £600 = £1,800. The allowance is better.
  • 2027–28: sales are under £3,000, so the allowance covers all of it. You do not need to register for this income, unless one of HMRC’s special cases applies to you.

Example 3: £3,200 of sales, £680 of costs

  • 2026–27: you need a tax return. With the allowance: £3,200 − £1,000 = £2,200 taxable profit. With actual costs: £3,200 − £680 = £2,520. The allowance is better.
  • 2027–28: sales are still over £3,000, so you still need a tax return. With the allowance: £3,200 − £3,000 = £200 taxable profit. With actual costs: £2,520. The allowance is much better.

Example 4: £18,000 of sales, £10,900 of costs

  • Both years: you need a tax return. With actual costs: £18,000 − £10,900 = £7,100 taxable profit.
  • With the allowance: £17,000 in 2026–27, £15,000 in 2027–28. Actual costs are better in both years, so the change does not help here.

Taxable profit is not the same as tax to pay. Income Tax applies only to income above your Personal Allowance, which is £12,570 as standard for 2026–27 (HMRC, Income Tax rates). The Personal Allowance applies to your total income, including any salary.

Platform reporting to HMRC

The policy paper does not mention digital platform reporting. Under the current rules on selling goods or services on a digital platform:

  • Since 1 January 2024, a platform may need to collect your details and report them to HMRC. It reports each calendar year by the following January.
  • A platform does not report your details if two conditions apply in a calendar year. You make fewer than 30 sales of goods, and you receive less than 2,000 euros (about £1,700) for those sales.
  • HMRC says the platform reports “do not replace your normal business records or tax calculations.”

Be careful: platform reports use the calendar year (January to December), but the allowance uses the tax year (6 April to 5 April). HMRC’s online platforms guidance says that if a platform shows calendar-year income, you need to calculate your income for the tax year yourself.

When does it become law?

The paper says: “Legislation in the Finance Bill 2026-27 will change the current law”. The final wording comes from that bill. On 12 November 2026, HMRC’s main guidance pages on the allowance and on online platforms still showed the £1,000 figures.

How many people it affects

HMRC’s estimates in the paper:

  • Up to 1.4 million people could potentially benefit.
  • Of those, about 400,000 pay less tax, on average £380 less.
  • About 100,000 no longer need to report their trading income to HMRC.

What to do now

  1. Add up your gross sales from stock you bought to resell since 6 April 2026.
  2. If the total for 2026–27 goes over £1,000, report it on a Self Assessment return. If you are not registered yet, register by 5 October 2027.
  3. Keep a record of each sale and each cost. HMRC’s examples of records include invoices, a spreadsheet of income receipts and bank statements.
  4. From 6 April 2027, check your 2027–28 sales against £3,000 instead.

Frequently asked questions

Is the trading allowance going up to £3,000?

Yes, from 6 April 2027, for the tax year 2027–28 onwards. The Self Assessment reporting threshold for trading income rises to £3,000 at the same time. HMRC published this in a policy paper with Budget 2026 on 28 October 2026. The paper says the change will be made in the Finance Bill 2026-27.

Does the £3,000 allowance apply to my 2026–27 sales?

No. For 2026–27 (6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027) the allowance is still £1,000. If your gross trading income for that year is more than £1,000, you need a Self Assessment return for it. If you are not registered yet, register by 5 October 2027.

Is the £3,000 based on sales or profit?

Sales. HMRC’s current guidance applies the allowance to gross income, before any expenses. The paper describes no change to this rule.

Can I use the £3,000 allowance and deduct my expenses?

No. Under HMRC’s current guidance you use the allowance or your actual expenses, not both. Pick the one that gives the lower taxable profit. The paper describes no change to this rule.

Do eBay and Vinted still report my sales to HMRC?

The paper does not mention platform reporting. Under the current rules, a platform may need to report your details to HMRC each year. A platform does not report your details if two conditions apply in a calendar year. You make fewer than 30 sales of goods, and you receive less than 2,000 euros (about £1,700) for those sales.
