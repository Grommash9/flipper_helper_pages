Vinted Pro UK: Do You Need a Business Account to Sell on Vinted?

11 November 2026 Guides 9 min read

The short answer: if you buy stock to resell on Vinted, Vinted says you need a Vinted Pro account. Pro is free and adds no seller fee. But there are trade-offs. Consumers can return items within 14 days, and your business details go on your profile. Also, you can only sell in set categories.

Pro and HMRC are separate questions. A Pro account doesn’t mean you owe tax, and a personal account doesn’t protect you from it. Below is what Vinted’s own Pro pages, help centre and Pro terms, and GOV.UK say, checked on 11 November 2026. For the buyer-side fees on Vinted, see our Vinted fees guide.

What is Vinted Pro?

Vinted Pro is Vinted’s account type for professional sellers. Vinted’s Pro guide says who can sell on Vinted as a professional. It says you can do so if you are registered as a sole trader, a non-profit organisation or a company in the UK.

Three things change when you go Pro:

  • A “Pro” badge appears next to your name on your profile and on your item pages. Buyers then know they are buying from a business (Vinted Help: Registering as a Pro seller).
  • Different terms apply. Your sales run under the Vinted Pro Terms and Conditions (the current version applies from 5 October 2026), in addition to the normal user terms.
  • Consumer law applies to you as a trader. The biggest practical effect is the 14-day return right, covered below.

Do I need Vinted Pro?

If you sell as a business, yes. The Vinted Pro page says: “If you want to sell a high volume of items on Vinted (more than just clearing out your closet!), you need to sign up as a professional seller.”

The Pro guide is stricter. It says a professional must declare themselves as a Pro seller. It adds that failure to do so can lead to sanctions under the Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013. It also says Vinted can ask you to create a Pro account, and can block your access if you meet the criteria and don’t register.

Vinted doesn’t give a number. The Vinted pages checked for this guide are the Pro page, the Pro guide, the registration help article and the Pro terms. None of them says how many sales or how much money makes you “high volume”. A useful test is HMRC’s own: GOV.UK says you are likely trading if you sell goods you bought intending to sell for a profit. Clearing out your own wardrobe is not trading. Buying at car boot sales to resell is.

Sellers report that Vinted acts on this without warning. One seller said Vinted flagged them as a commercial seller with only about 40 listings of skirts and trousers. That’s one seller’s report, not a Vinted rule. But it shows that Vinted decides who is a professional seller, not you.

Vinted Pro fees: what it costs

Vinted Pro is free. The Pro page FAQ says: “Yes, Vinted Pro is entirely free. With Vinted Pro, you can list as many second-hand items as you like at no cost.”

The money flow is the same as on a personal account. The Pro terms say the buyer pays the item price, the shipping fee, the Vinted fee and any optional services. The Vinted fee is “a mandatory platform fee, payable by buyers”. For how Vinted calculates that fee, see our Vinted fees breakdown. Payouts from your Vinted Wallet to your bank are free of transfer fees under the same terms.

Two costs are new for a Pro seller:

  • VAT, if you are registered. The Pro terms say you must include VAT in the total price if it applies to you. The Pro guide’s VAT section says selling on Vinted does not by itself mean you must register. You must register when your taxable turnover goes over the threshold. GOV.UK gives the threshold as £90,000 over the last 12 months. Sales outside Vinted count towards it.
  • Returns. There is no fee, but returned items cost you time and sometimes postage. Include that cost in your prices.

How to sign up for Vinted Pro in the UK

From Vinted’s registration help article:

  1. Choose your route. If you’re new to Vinted, sign up from the Vinted Pro page. If you already use Vinted, you can convert your account. Or you can keep it and open a separate Pro account with a different email address and phone number. You may have one standard and one Pro account.
  2. Clear your orders first. You can’t convert a standard account while you have ongoing orders. Wait until all orders are complete and your balance is withdrawn.
  3. Give your business details. Sole traders give a National Insurance number, a VAT number (if they have one), business representative details and a business address. Charities and companies give representative details, the registered business name, address, Tax Identification Number and Business Registration Number.
  4. Set up your profile with a display name and contact details.
  5. Withdraw any earnings from your current Vinted Balance. Vinted creates a new balance for the Pro account. The money reaches your bank within 5 business days.
  6. Pass the checks. Upload the documents Vinted asks for in its Know Your Customer / Know Your Business process. The mandatory information article lists a copy of your ID (passport, ID card or driving licence) and bank details among them.
  7. Add your own policies (if any) in your policies settings. Buyers see them on your profile and item pages.

You can’t change some details later in your Pro settings. One example is your Tax Identification Number. You need to contact Vinted for that, so make sure they are correct the first time.

Buyer returns: the 14-day rule

This is the change that matters most to a reseller. On Pro, a buyer can return an item just because they changed their mind.

Section 17 of the Vinted Pro terms says a consumer who buys from a Pro seller can withdraw from the purchase without giving any reason. The right comes from the Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013. The key rules:

  • The buyer has 14 calendar days, counted from the day after delivery, to say they want to return the item. They then have another 14 days to send it back with tracking.
  • The buyer usually pays return shipping, unless your own policy says you will.
  • On a returned single item, the buyer gets a full refund of the item price, the Vinted fee and the original shipping fee. Return postage is taken off their refund if they paid it.
  • Exceptions include items made to order or personalised, sealed hygiene items opened after delivery, and sealed audio, video or software opened after delivery.
  • Vinted may let a buyer keep the item without returning it in some cases, for example where there is strong evidence it is counterfeit.

There is a cash-flow effect too. The Pro terms say what happens if the buyer doesn’t report an issue within the 14-day “Pro Return Period”. The order completes automatically, and the money in escrow goes to you. You must also send each item within 5 business days, or the order is cancelled automatically.

What shows on your Vinted Pro profile

The Pro page FAQ lists what is visible on your profile. Besides your email address and Pro seller policies, your profile shows your company name, company number, contact details, trade registrar and business address.

It adds that sole traders can choose to keep these details private during registration. Compare this with the Pro guide. The guide lists details that you must give to buyers, including your name and the address of your establishment. The guide says buyers must get these details on your profile, in your additional policies and in the order confirmation email. If you sell from home and want your address private, check what buyers will see before you start selling.

What you can sell on Vinted Pro

Pro has a shorter category list than a personal account. From the Pro page, you can sell:

  • Women’s, men’s and children’s clothing, footwear and accessories
  • Children’s toys, furniture and childcare equipment
  • Tech accessories (such as headphones, smart watches and phone cases)
  • Homeware (textiles, tableware, home accessories, seasonal and party decorations)
  • Entertainment (books, consoles and games, board games and puzzles, music and video)
  • Pet care items

Antiques, collectables and most general electronics are not on that list. If those are your stock, Vinted Pro won’t take them, and eBay is the obvious place to sell them.

There are extra rules for each listing. The mandatory information article says Pro sellers must give the manufacturer’s details and labelling information for many categories. Examples are the fabric composition for clothing and shoes, CE marking and safety warnings for kids’ toys, and the PEGI rating for video games. It also says that Pro sellers must not sell cosmetics.

The Pro terms say you sell second-hand items. They also say that items must not be something you bought in bulk or as a dropship product with a low resale value. You also need photos and descriptions you made yourself, not ones taken from the internet.

Vinted Pro, HMRC and platform reporting

Vinted Pro is a Vinted account setting. It doesn’t register you with HMRC, and it doesn’t decide whether you owe tax. The Pro guide says you are solely responsible for registering your business and meeting your tax obligations.

Trading and the £1,000 allowance

If you buy to resell, HMRC treats that as likely trading. You add together your trading income from all sources. If the total is over the £1,000 trading allowance for the tax year (6 April to 5 April), you need to tell HMRC. GOV.UK says the allowance applies to gross income, before expenses. It also says you must register for Self Assessment by 5 October after the tax year. Our trading allowance guide for resellers explains when to claim the allowance and when to deduct real costs instead.

Digital platform reporting

Since 1 January 2024, UK digital platform reporting rules require platforms to collect seller details and send them to HMRC by the following January. Platforms must give you a copy of what they report. For an individual, the details include your name, home address, date of birth and National Insurance number.

A platform does not report you if two conditions apply in the calendar year. You make fewer than 30 sales of goods, and you receive less than 2,000 euros (about £1,700). These rules cover platform sellers in general, so a platform can report a personal Vinted account too.

HMRC is clear that a report is not a tax bill. GOV.UK says a platform reporting your details “does not automatically mean you owe tax”. HMRC’s December 2024 statement says there is no tax due if you are not trading and just occasionally sell unwanted items. One more detail: the report covers a calendar year, but your tax return covers 6 April to 5 April. So the two figures won’t match.

For eBay income on top of Vinted, see eBay selling and tax in the UK.

Pros and cons of Vinted Pro for resellers

Pros

  • No new fee. Your margin per sale stays the same as on a personal account.
  • You stop risking the account. Vinted says it can block professionals who don’t register. A Pro account removes that risk.
  • Buyers know who they’re dealing with. The badge and your policies make you look like a proper shop.
  • You can keep your personal account for your own clear-outs, with a separate email and phone number.

Cons

  • 14-day returns. Expect some returns, and include their cost in your prices.
  • Public business details, including an address, unless you are a sole trader and choose privacy.
  • Fewer categories. No antiques or collectables, and no cosmetics.
  • More work per listing in categories that need composition, manufacturer details or CE marking.
  • Strict tool rules. The Pro terms ban external software tools such as bots unless Vinted authorises them. They also ban deleting and relisting the same item many times or in bulk. If you cross-list with automation, check this first.

My opinion: if you’re buying stock to sell for profit, go Pro, and go Pro on purpose rather than after a suspension. Before you do, list what you sell by category and check it against the Pro list. If most of your stock is clothing and homeware, little changes except returns. If it is collectables, Vinted Pro is the wrong place for it.

Frequently asked questions

What is Vinted Pro?

Vinted Pro is Vinted’s account type for professional sellers. In the UK, sole traders, charities and registered companies can join. Pro sellers get a “Pro” badge on their profile and items. They sell under the Vinted Pro Terms and Conditions and must let consumers return items within 14 days.

Do I need Vinted Pro?

If you sell as a business, yes. Vinted says you need to sign up as a professional seller if you want to sell a high volume of items. Vinted describes this as more than just clearing out your wardrobe. Its Pro guide says professionals must declare themselves as Pro sellers. It also says Vinted can block your access if you meet the criteria and don’t register. Vinted doesn’t publish a number of sales that makes you a professional.

How much does Vinted Pro cost? Are there Vinted Pro fees?

Vinted Pro is free. Vinted says you can list as many second-hand items as you like at no cost. As on a personal account, the buyer pays the Vinted fee and shipping at checkout. If you are VAT-registered, your listing price must include VAT.

Can I have a Vinted Pro account and a personal account?

Yes. You can have one standard account and one Pro account, but each needs a different email address and phone number. You can also convert your existing account to Pro, but not while you have ongoing orders. Wait until all orders are complete and your balance is withdrawn first.

Can buyers return items to a Vinted Pro seller?

Yes. A consumer who buys from a Pro seller has a legal right to withdraw within 14 calendar days of delivery, without giving a reason. The buyer usually pays return shipping unless your own policy says otherwise. On a refund, the buyer gets back the item price, the Vinted fee and the original shipping fee. Some items are exempt, such as personalised items and unsealed hygiene items.

Does Vinted report my sales to HMRC?

Under UK digital platform reporting rules, which started on 1 January 2024, platforms collect seller details and report them to HMRC by the following January. You aren’t reported if you make fewer than 30 sales of goods and receive less than 2,000 euros (about £1,700) in the year. HMRC says a report does not automatically mean you owe tax. These rules apply to platform sellers in general, not only to Pro accounts.
