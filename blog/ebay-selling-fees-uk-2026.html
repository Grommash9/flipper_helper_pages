eBay Selling Fees UK 2026: Complete Breakdown for Private and Business Sellers

17 April 2026 Guides 9 min read

If you sell on eBay UK, the fee structure changed again in February 2026 and it confuses a lot of people. The biggest thing most sellers miss: private sellers now pay zero final value fees. If you live in the UK and sell your own things as a private seller, eBay charges no final value fee when your item sells.

Business sellers still pay final value fees (the eBay final value fee is the main cost), and those went up slightly in some categories. Here’s a breakdown of how much eBay charges to sell — whether you’re a private or business seller. If you just want to calculate your fees quickly, use our eBay fee calculator.

Private sellers: it’s free to sell

Since 1 October 2024, UK-based private sellers pay no final value fees and no regulatory operating fees. This applies to all categories except motors (cars, motorcycles, vehicles).

What you get as a private seller:

  • 300 free listings per month (400 with an eBay Shop subscription)
  • No final value fee when your item sells
  • No regulatory operating fee

Private sellers pay fees in these cases:

  • 35p per listing if you go over your 300 free monthly allowance
  • 3% international fee if the buyer’s delivery address is outside the UK, unless you use eBay International Shipping
  • Optional listing upgrades such as subtitles or Promoted Listings

Sources: eBay UK, Fees for private sellers; eBay UK, Selling on eBay FAQs

The catch: buyers pay a Buyer Protection fee

When someone buys from a UK-based private seller, eBay includes a Buyer Protection fee in the price the buyer sees. You don’t pay it. When buyers buy from a business seller, they pay no Buyer Protection fee. The fee (including VAT) is:

  • 10p flat per item, plus
  • 7% of the item price up to £20, plus
  • 4% of the part from £20 to £300, plus
  • 2% of the part from £300 to £4,000 (nothing above £4,000)

For example, on a £25 jacket the fee is £1.70 (10p + £1.40 + 20p). On a £5 item, the fee is 45p (10p + 35p). Think about this fee when you set your price.

Source: eBay UK, Buyer Protection fee

Business sellers: final value fees by category

If you’re registered as a business seller on eBay UK, you pay a final value fee on every sale. This is a percentage of the total sale amount (item price + postage + any applicable taxes).

As of November 2026, the main reseller categories and their fees are:

Category Final value fee (excl. VAT)
Clothes, Shoes & Accessories 11.9% (women’s bags 12.9% up to £800)
Home, Furniture & DIY 11.9% up to £500, 7.9% above
Toys & Games 10.9%
Antiques, Collectables, Pottery & Glass 10.9%
Books, Comics & Magazines 9.9%
Films & TV, Music, Video Games 9.9% (consoles 6.9% up to £400)
Sporting Goods 10.9%
Jewellery & Watches 14.9% up to £1,000, 4% above
Mobile & Smart Phones 6.9% up to £1,000, 3% above
Business, Office & Industrial 12.5% (was 11.9% before Feb 2026)
Vehicle Parts & Accessories 9.5% up to £750, 3% above
Wholesale & Job Lots, Everything Else 12.9%

eBay lists business fees excluding VAT. eBay adds VAT to its fees where VAT applies. The UK standard rate of VAT is 20%. With 20% VAT, an 11.9% final value fee costs about 14.3% of the sale. If you’re VAT-registered you can usually reclaim that VAT.

For the complete category list, check eBay UK — Fees for business sellers (last updated by eBay on 5 November 2026).

Sources: eBay UK, Fees for business sellers; eBay UK, Tax policy; GOV.UK, VAT rates; GOV.UK, Charge, reclaim and record VAT

Per-order fee (the bit that changed in February 2026)

The business seller final value fee has two parts: a percentage and a fixed per-order fee. The per-order fee is:

  • Orders of £10 or less: 30p per order
  • Orders over £10: 40p per order (was 30p before 12 February 2026)

In some categories, the per-order fee is 10p on orders of £10 or less. These categories are Home, Furniture & DIY, Antiques, Art, Coins, Collectables, Dolls & Bears, Pottery, Ceramics & Glass, Sports Memorabilia and Stamps. The 10p fee is for business sellers with a UK registered address. It applies to listings created from 1 February 2022 (from 19 April 2022 in Home, Furniture & DIY).

The per-order fee is small on high-value items. On cheaper items, it is a larger part of the price.

Sources: eBay UK Seller Centre, Rate card change; eBay UK, Fees for business sellers; eBay UK, 10p per-order fee in Home, Furniture & DIY; eBay UK, 10p per-order fee in selected categories

Regulatory operating fee

Business sellers also pay a 0.35% regulatory operating fee on all sales. eBay says the fee addresses the rising cost of regulations, for example consumer protection, environmental protection, tax and customs rules. eBay calculates it on the total sale amount, including postage.

UK-based private sellers don’t pay this fee.

Sources: eBay UK Seller Centre, Seller fees; eBay UK, Fees for business sellers; eBay UK, Fees for private sellers

Less common fees worth knowing

  • Dispute fee: if you’re found responsible for a chargeback or other disputed payment, eBay charges £16.80 including VAT (£14 + VAT for business sellers).
  • Currency conversion: eBay can convert your money when you list or sell on another eBay site (for example, eBay.de). If it does, it keeps a conversion charge of 2.5%. From 1 December 2026, the charge rises to 2.75%.

Sources: eBay UK, Fees for private sellers; eBay UK, Fees for business sellers

Listing fees (insertion fees)

eBay’s help pages call these listing fees. eBay’s Seller Centre calls them insertion fees. eBay charges them when you create a listing, whether or not the item sells.

  • Private sellers: 300 free listings/month (400 with a Shop subscription), then 35p per listing
  • Business sellers without a Shop: no free allowance — 30p + VAT per fixed-price or auction listing
  • Business sellers with a Shop: a free allowance by tier, then 10p, 5p or free per extra fixed-price listing (see the Shop table below)

For private sellers, a listing with multiple quantities or variations counts as one listing.

Business sellers must be careful with Good ’Til Cancelled listings. eBay charges the listing fee again every month the listing renews. It also charges the fee every time you relist an item. Without a Shop, an unsold Good ’Til Cancelled listing costs a business seller 36p a month (30p + 20% VAT).

Private sellers don’t pay again when eBay renews or relists a listing automatically. If you relist an item manually, it uses one of your free listings, or costs 35p after you use all your free listings.

Sources: eBay UK, Fees for private sellers; eBay UK, Fees for business sellers; eBay UK Seller Centre, Seller fees

International fee

If a buyer’s delivery address is outside the UK, you pay an international fee on the total sale amount:

  • Private sellers: 3% (all international destinations)
  • Business sellers: 1.05% (Eurozone and Northern Europe: Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Norway and Sweden), 1.8% (US & Canada), 2.0% (all other countries)

If your registered address is in the UK, you won’t pay it when you post abroad with eBay International Shipping on eligible listings. You can also exclude international delivery locations from your listings. The business rates exclude VAT.

Sources: eBay UK, Fees for private sellers; eBay UK, Fees for business sellers

eBay Shop subscriptions

These are the business Shop tiers on eBay’s fee page (prices exclude VAT). Private sellers can also subscribe to a Shop for £19.99 a month including VAT. They then get 100 extra free listings a month.

Plan Monthly price Free listings/month Extra fixed-price listing
No Shop — None 30p
Basic £27 250 fixed-price + 100 auction 10p
Featured £77 1,500 fixed-price + 600 auction 5p
Anchor £437 Unlimited fixed-price + 1,000 auction Free

A private seller who creates 300 listings a month or fewer pays no listing fees without a Shop. For a business seller, compare listing fees only: without a Shop, 90 listings cost £27 (90 × 30p). So Basic costs less on listing fees only when you create more than 90 listings a month.

Sources: eBay UK, Fees for business sellers; eBay UK, Fees for private sellers

Worked example: what does eBay actually take?

For example, you buy a jacket at a car boot sale for £5. You sell it on eBay for £25 and charge the buyer £4.50 for postage.

As a private seller

  • Final value fee: £0 (private sellers sell free)
  • Listing fee: £0 (within your 300 free listings)
  • eBay takes: £0
  • Your revenue: £29.50 (item + postage)
  • After purchase price and postage cost: your profit depends on your actual Royal Mail/Evri cost

As a business seller (Clothes, Shoes & Accessories at 11.9%)

  • Total sale: £29.50 (item + postage)
  • Final value fee: £29.50 × 11.9% = £3.51
  • Per-order fee: £0.40 (order over £10)
  • Regulatory operating fee: £29.50 × 0.35% = £0.10
  • VAT on fees (20%): £0.80
  • Total eBay takes: £4.81
  • Your revenue: £24.69

These figures do not include the £5 purchase price, the real postage cost, packaging, or any entry fees or transport from sourcing. For example, say postage costs you the same £4.50 that the buyer paid. Then your profit is £15.19 as a business seller (£24.69 − £5 − £4.50). As a private seller, it is £20.00 (£29.50 − £5 − £4.50). Packaging and transport costs reduce this further.

Remember that eBay’s business seller policy says you must register as a business if, for example, you sell items you bought to resell. If you bought the jacket to resell it, the business seller figures apply to you.

This is why you need to know your real profit after all expenses. Use our eBay fee calculator to calculate the fees for an item before you list it.

Sources: eBay UK, Fees for business sellers; eBay UK, Business seller policy

How do eBay fees compare with other platforms?

Platform Seller fees
eBay UK (private seller) No final value fee (buyer pays a Buyer Protection fee)
eBay UK (business seller) About 16-17% total on a £20-30 clothing sale (final value + per-order + regulatory + VAT)
Vinted 0% seller fee (buyer pays a “Vinted fee”, usually 3-8% + 30-80p)
Depop 0% selling fee for UK sellers (seller pays 2.9% + 30p payment processing)
Gumtree Most services free (optional paid ad promotions)

UK-based private sellers pay no final value fee on eBay, and Vinted charges sellers no selling fee. On both platforms, the buyer pays a fee instead. Private seller vs business seller: which am I?

eBay says a private account is the best option if you sell casually, for example items you no longer want. eBay says you need a business account if you sell large quantities, or sell items you made or bought to resell. You also need one if you already have a business outside eBay.

eBay’s business seller policy says you must register as a business if, for example, you sell items you bought to resell.

HMRC has its own rules. You must register for Self Assessment if your gross trading income is over £1,000 in a tax year. For more, see our £1,000 trading allowance guide.

Sources: eBay UK Seller Centre, Business accounts; eBay UK, Business seller policy; GOV.UK, Tax-free allowances on property and trading income

Frequently asked questions

How much does eBay charge to sell in the UK?

UK-based private sellers pay no final value fee, and listing is free up to 300 listings per month. Business sellers pay 9.9-12.9% (excl. VAT) in most categories (11.9% for clothes). They also pay a per-order fee (30p or 40p in most categories) and a 0.35% regulatory operating fee.

Do private sellers pay fees on eBay UK?

Usually not. Since 1 October 2024, UK-based private sellers pay no final value fees. Fees apply if you list more than 300 items in a month, add optional upgrades, or post to an overseas address. Your buyer pays a Buyer Protection fee instead.

What changed with eBay UK fees in February 2026?

On 12 February 2026, the per-order fee went from 30p to 40p on orders over £10 for business sellers. Final value fees also went up in some categories, for example Business, Office & Industrial (from 11.9% to 12.5%).

Is it free to list on eBay UK?

For private sellers, yes: 300 free listings/month, then 35p each. Business sellers without a Shop get no free listings and pay 30p + VAT per listing; Shop tiers include free listings.

What is the eBay insertion fee in the UK?

eBay charges the insertion fee when you create a listing. eBay’s help pages call it a listing fee. Private sellers get 300 free listings a month, then pay 35p each. Business sellers without an eBay Shop pay 30p + VAT per listing. Business sellers pay the fee again every time they relist and every month a Good ’Til Cancelled listing renews.

What is the eBay Buyer Protection fee?

It’s a fee the buyer pays when buying from a UK private seller. The fee is 10p per item plus 7% of the price up to £20. The fee also includes 4% of the part from £20 to £300 and 2% of the part from £300 to £4,000. These amounts include VAT. The seller doesn’t pay it, but it is included in the price buyers see.

How much does an eBay business account cost?

Without an eBay Shop there’s no monthly charge (eBay calls it “pay as you sell”). You pay per sale: a final value fee by category (11.9% for clothes) and a per-order fee (30p or 40p in most categories). You also pay a 0.35% regulatory operating fee. eBay adds VAT to these fees. Without a Shop, each listing costs 30p + VAT. Shop subscriptions cost £27 (Basic), £77 (Featured) or £437 (Anchor) a month, excluding VAT.

How does eBay managed payments work?

Buyers can pay with many payment methods, for example cards, Apple Pay, Google Pay, PayPal and PayPal Credit. eBay deducts selling fees from your sales proceeds before they appear in your account. You get the rest in your linked bank account. Business sellers get daily payouts by default. A payout can take up to 4 business days to clear. Private sellers usually get funds in their eBay balance 2 days after tracked delivery. They can then withdraw the funds to their bank.

What is the eBay final value fee?

The final value fee is the main selling fee for eBay business sellers. It’s a percentage of the total sale amount (item price + postage) plus a per-order fee. eBay deducts it when your item sells. Most categories are between 9.9% and 12.9% (clothes 11.9%, books 9.9%, toys 10.9%). Phones and consoles have lower rates, and jewellery has rates up to 14.9%. UK-based private sellers don’t pay final value fees, except on Cars, Motorcycles & Vehicles listings.

Are eBay fees worth it compared to Vinted or Facebook Marketplace?

If you’re a UK-based private seller, eBay charges no final value fee, and Vinted charges sellers no selling fee. As a business seller on eBay, you pay about 16–17% on a £20–30 clothing sale. In that case, the answer depends on whether eBay brings you enough extra sales to be worth the fees. For Facebook, see our guide to selling on Facebook Marketplace.
